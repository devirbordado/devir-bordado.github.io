There are stories that continue to accompany us long after they end. Not because they have all the answers, but because, as we change, they also seem to say different things.

That is how it happens with me and Doctor Who. (I am a Whovian—this is my favorite series of all time!)

But I return to the series from time to time and, curiously, I never return to rewatch the same episodes in the same way. I return to rediscover questions that still make sense for who I have become.

Among all the companions who traveled in the TARDIS, Clara Oswald remains my favorite. Not because she never made mistakes. I like her because, every time I rewatch her story, I notice a very specific choice that goes unnoticed by many people.

Clara never ceased to exist in order to live the Doctor’s life.

It may seem like a small detail, but it is one of the greatest lessons of the series.

While other companions embark immediately on the adventure, Clara imposes a very simple limit: she continues living her own life. She has a profession, students, commitments, and a routine that does not disappear when the TARDIS lands at her door.

The Doctor becomes part of her life. He does not become her life.

When I first watched the series, many years ago, I remember that a good part of the fandom seemed bothered by this stance. Clara was often criticized. I myself read various texts trying to understand why she provoked such intense reactions.

Today I think that perhaps the discomfort was precisely there.

We are used to stories in which love requires abandonment. A character meets someone extraordinary and, from that instant, everything that existed before loses importance: friends, work, dreams, projects… everything begins to orbit a single person.

Clara does the opposite she demonstrates that it is possible to live a great adventure without abandoning your own story.

Rose Tyler represents another moment in life. She reminds me of those first loves in which everything seems urgent, absolute, and inevitable. When we are capable of reorganizing the entire world around someone because we believe that it will last forever.

Who has never experienced something like that?

Perhaps that is why Rose is such a beloved character. She translates a profoundly human experience: the intensity of someone who is still discovering love.

Clara, on the other hand, seems to speak of another stage.

The one in which we understand that loving someone does not need to mean disappearing inside that person’s life. There is an enormous difference between sharing a path and abandoning your own path.

The TARDIS was always presented as a machine capable of crossing time and space. But, looking today, I think the greatest journeys of the series never happened between planets. They happened inside people.

Each regeneration changes the Doctor. Each trip transforms whoever enters that blue cabin. Each farewell leaves marks.

Traveling is also allowing yourself to change without completely losing who you are.

I am deeply passionate about this series because, deep down, I always wanted to flee, just as the Doctor flees from Gallifrey. Throughout life, all of us cross through many TARDISes. We change cities. We change professions. We enter and leave relationships. We build friendships. We lose important people. We discover new interests. We abandon old certainties.

I have lived all of that, and some of those things more than once, and others I am living again for a third time. But none of those crossings should require us to leave our identity behind.

Of course, relationships transform us. Thank goodness they do. The problem begins when transformation ceases to be growth and becomes erasure.

The greatest lesson Clara left me is not to refuse adventures, but to know that there will always be a life waiting when the TARDIS door closes.

And maturing is precisely learning that balance.

Continuing to accept the invitations that life offers, to know new places, new people, and new versions of ourselves, without forgetting who entered the cabin before the journey began.

Because some crossings change everything. But none of them should make us disappear.

It is crossing through an entire life without ceasing to recognize your own story.

And it is about herself. It always was. My impossible girl!
