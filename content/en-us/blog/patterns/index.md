As I have already shared here, my Pallas is retrograde in Pisces.

This makes me a person who “learns through symbologies” and who processes life’s questions first internally and only later, once I have reflected, externalizes the thoughts.

I think it is important to begin this text with that statement because I have been weaving reflections for a long time, and I want to talk about some advice I received after looking at it from another angle.

I will speak of tarot again, using it only as an example, because all decks used for divinatory purposes are anchored in universal symbols that dialogue with the human psyche. They end up conversing with our existential crises through doors that other tools cannot always enter.

Around mid-July, I received a single-card draw from an alternative deck. The **Spider – Patterns** card came out.

The statement of this card, within the oracle in which it was created, says that the person is repeating behavioral, relational, psychological, or emotional patterns that keep them trapped in the same web, preventing their progress.

It brings a warning to reflect on attitudes and on situations that have been repeating. It is a valid interpretation. I believe it makes a lot of sense within the archetype of the search for healing and evolution. The symbolism is very strong.

So, if that was the message I received, why did I keep reflecting on it for months?

Well then.

The card, by itself, isolated from the rest of the “tarological” reading (I know that word doesn’t exist, but it very well could!), made sense. However, when observed within the complete spread, it no longer did as much.

I looked at that spread, at all the symbols that composed it, and I realized there was another narrative being presented. A narrative that not even the card reader had perceived at the moment of the reading.

The first level of reading this card is exactly the level she accessed: it speaks of mental patterns and of observing what repeats. And, within this very initial interpretation, there is already an implicit invitation to look at the whole, and not to draw conclusions based on a single card. See the message that repeats. See the patterns presented by the draw.

On a second level, however, the card shows a spider web and the spider itself upon it. And what does the spider do?

She weaves.

She does not force. She does not chase the prey. She creates a structure and then waits.

The spider is not trapped in the patterns of the web—she is the mistress of the web.

And this completely changes the interpretation. Do you see?

It was not about modifying what I was doing, but about recognizing that precisely what I had been doing was what was building a solid structure.

Curiously, the spider also appears here as an archetype of research.

She weaves threads.

A researcher does exactly the same: weaves countless invisible threads that, little by little, connect until they form a great web of knowledge.

That little card, so simple, is much broader than just the basic meaning attributed to it. For me, it dialogued with a question that had not even been asked. It spoke of my professional future, although that was not the central question of the reading.

That is, the spread answered even what had not been asked.

Later we see memes about great Hollywood personalities using tarot as a tool for self-knowledge and decision-making, and people do not understand the reason for so much enthusiasm.

Adding this card to the rest of the spread, the message I received was another:

Keep weaving your web. Do not try to control who enters it, and do not adapt yourself to please everyone, because what needs to arrive will find the right threads to you.

See how different this is from the initial interpretation, although it does not contradict it. On the contrary, it expands its meaning. But this understanding did not come at the moment. It required time and internal processing.

What do I want by sharing this experience?

It is not even to encourage you to do a tarot reading, although I recommend you try one day, even if out of curiosity. But, if you do, ask for a photo of the spread. Many aspects only reveal themselves with the passage of time, because a draw often answers far beyond the question formulated.

My goal is another.

I want to say that we should always grant experiences the benefit of the doubt and of reinterpretation. Sometimes, we receive a narrative and believe in it with absolute conviction. But is it the only possible one?

Think of an example from scientific research.

A researcher collects an excellent set of data, but proposes no theory or produces a limited interpretation. Years later, another researcher revisits exactly the same data and produces a much more consistent explanation. The data never changed. What changed was the interpretation.

Do you see how this is not only about academic research?

It is about life itself.

I am not invalidating the advice I received. It remains extremely pertinent. I really am changing many things in myself. And one of those changes is precisely not being satisfied with only the first interpretation.

The very cards of the spread told me exactly this, without beating around the bush. In sequence, they advised discernment (King of Swords), a little cunning (Fox), deep investigation (Well), and, finally, the return to what is truly solid (House).

That is exactly it.

This text is Meraki. I write to leave you the same advice I received, because it was too good to remain only with me.
