We’ve already made the tea. The warm cup in hand, the smell of cinnamon throughout the house. But tea alone is sad. It needs a cake.

A simple cake the kind you cut a slice of and sit in the kitchen to chat. Soft on the inside, with pieces of apple that fall apart, and that Brazil nut that’s a little crunchy, a little soft, just as I remember eating at Casa de Bolos.

Shall we learn?

Before getting your hands in the dough, a brief explanation of what each ingredient does in the cake. It’s not magic it’s chemistry.

| Ingredient | Technical function |
|---|---|
| **Apple** | Moisture, natural sweetness, soft texture |
| **Brazil nut** | Crunchiness, good fat (selenium) |
| **Cinnamon** | Aroma, warmth, enhances apple flavor |
| **Flour** | Structure, body |
| **Sugar** | Sweetness, tenderness |
| **Egg** | Binds ingredients, provides structure |
| **Butter** | Tenderness, moisture, flavor |

Tip: use apples with the skin on (rich in fiber and antioxidants) and raw, unsalted nuts.

---

**Apple cake with Brazil nuts**

**Ingredients:**

| Ingredient | Quantity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 2 cups (280g) | Sifted |
| Sugar | 1½ cups (300g) | Can use brown sugar, it stays more moist |
| Vegetable oil | ¾ cup (180ml) | Corn, canola, sunflower. Do not use olive oil |
| Eggs | 3 units | At room temperature |
| Apple | 3 medium units | With skin, seeds removed, cut into small cubes |
| Brazil nuts | 1 cup (100g) | Raw, coarsely chopped (medium pieces) |
| Ground cinnamon | 1 tablespoon (heaped) | For the batter + a little to sprinkle on top at the end |
| Baking powder | 1 tablespoon | For cake (not baking soda) |
| Salt | 1 pinch | Enhances sweetness |

**Utensils:** Bundt pan (the kind with a central hole), greased and floured. Large bowl, hand whisk (can be a wire whisk), spatula, sieve.

**Method — step by step, calmly**

1. **Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F)** — medium temperature. Grease the pan by rubbing butter and dusting with flour.
2. **In a large bowl, beat the eggs with the sugar** until it becomes a light, fluffy cream. You can do this by hand (whisk) or with an electric mixer. The point is: beat for about 3–4 minutes until the volume increases and the color lightens.
3. **Add the oil little by little**, beating constantly. The batter will become glossy and smooth.
4. **Sift the flour together with the cinnamon, baking powder, and salt.** Add to the wet mixture and stir gently—do not overmix, or the cake will become tough. The goal is simply to incorporate.
5. **Add the chopped apple and chopped Brazil nuts.** Fold them in with a spatula, distributing the pieces evenly throughout the batter.
6. **Pour into the greased pan.**
7. **Bake for 40 to 50 minutes.** Test with a toothpick: insert it into the center of the cake; if it comes out clean or with few moist crumbs, it’s ready.
8. **Remove from the oven.** Let cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack. Sprinkle a little ground cinnamon on top (this is the only “decoration”—no frosting, no filling).

**Let cool completely before cutting.**

**What to do with leftovers (if any)**

- **Buttered toast:** Slice a piece, spread butter on both sides, and toast in a skillet until golden. Crispy on the outside, soft on the inside. A breakfast fit for the gods.
- **Sweet crumble:** Crumble the stale cake, mix with a little sugar and cinnamon, bake until dry, becomes a crumble topping for fruits or yogurt.
- **Tea accompaniment:** Obviously, with the apple cinnamon tea I already taught you [here](/en-us/blog/recipe-apple-cinnamon-tea/).
